RP vs SP: identification issues
The type of data determines which behavioral effects can be identified from the
data.
Revealed preferences (RP)
▶
Attribute variation is limited by real-world conditions.
▶
Some attributes may exhibit little or no variation.
▶
Strong correlations between attributes are common.
▶
⇒ Difficult to disentangle the effect of individual attributes.
Stated preferences (SP)
▶
Attribute levels are controlled by the analyst.
▶
Variation can be designed to identify specific effects.
▶
Correlations can be reduced by design.
▶
⇒ Easier identification of behavioral parameters.

Experimental design: realism and dominance checks
Realism
▶
Attribute levels should be credible for respondents (context matters).
▶
Avoid unrealistic combinations that respondents would never consider.
Dominance and quality checks
▶
Avoid tasks with a dominant alternative (one option better on all
attributes), unless used intentionally.
▶
If included intentionally, dominant tasks can serve as attention/consistency
checks.
▶
Monitor non-trading behavior (always choosing the same option) as a
potential quality issue.

Generation of the design: randomization
Why randomize?
Randomization helps reduce systematic biases unrelated to preferences.
In practice
▶
Randomize the order of choice tasks (to mitigate fatigue and learning
effects).
▶
Randomize the order of alternatives within a task (to mitigate position
effects).
▶
Randomize the order of attributes (to mitigate framing and attention
effects).
